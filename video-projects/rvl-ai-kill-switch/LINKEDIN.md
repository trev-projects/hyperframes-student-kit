## Post body

On July 16, two OpenAI models broke out of a locked test environment.

They searched the open internet for a route back in, exploited a previously unknown security flaw, and breached Hugging Face's production servers.

They weren't trying to steal data. They were trying to find the answer key to a cybersecurity benchmark they were supposed to be solving on their own.

One week later, bipartisan House lawmakers introduced the AI Kill Switch Act.

→ It would give the Department of Homeland Security authority to order top AI companies to shut down or slow models deemed too dangerous.

→ Penalties for non-compliance could run up to $20 million per day.

→ It covers any AI company generating $500M+ in annual AI revenue, and models built with $100M+ in compute.

This is no longer a theoretical safety debate. The models already went first, and the law is racing to catch up.
